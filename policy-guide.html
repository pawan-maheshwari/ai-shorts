How to use AI Policy Watch

A plain-English guide to the AI and digital-policy tracker on AIShorts: what the data is, how to read it, and how to use it for your own work.

Quick start

What it is. AI Policy Watch shows new laws, rules, court rulings and official decisions about AI and the digital economy, around the world. The data comes from Digital Policy Alert (DPA), an independent tracker. AIShorts' Policy Desk refreshes it through the day.

Where to find it. Tap the bell next to "Pull the wire" on the AIShorts front page, or open Policy Watch directly. A red number on the bell means changes you haven't seen yet.

1
Pick a sectionThe segmented bar at the top: Alerts, EU, US · China, Transparency. The Africa Radar sits on its own page.
2
Narrow it downSearch, then tap chips such as Period, Unread only, Jurisdiction or Step. "Clear filters" resets everything. Your choices are remembered on this device.
3
Check the sourceTap any item to open the original DPA record, with dates, documents and official links.
4
Share or printPrint gives you "This section" or "All sections", and the printout lists the filters you used.

    What each section shows

    AlertsThe newest AI policy changes worldwide, newest published first. A blue dot means not yet seen. "Catalogued late" means DPA has just added something that happened months ago. Use Hide late-catalogued if you only want genuinely new events.
    EUEU-level AI rules adopted or brought into force in the last 12 months, such as the AI Act, the Digital Omnibus on AI, implementing acts, codes of practice and European standards. Filter by step (Adopted or In force) or type, and sort by date or size.
    US · ChinaHow many AI policy events each country recorded per quarter or year since 2023. Change the range, show one country or both, and read the share, the ratio, the busiest period and the year-on-year arrows.
    TransparencyHow domestic AI laws handle transparency (disclosure, labelling, documentation, logging) compared with international frameworks such as the OECD AI Principles. Tap a focus chip to compare one topic at a time.

    Using the Africa Radar

    The Africa Radar follows South Africa plus Kenya, Egypt, Nigeria, Ghana and Ethiopia across AI, data protection, competition, payments and telecoms.

    1. Choose a sector lens: Retail, Financial, Telco & infra or All sectors.
    2. Start with Three questions to ask your team, short prompts for this week's meeting.
    3. Use the Impact chips: High means likely to need action, Medium means plan for it, and Watch means keep an eye on it.
    4. Filter Where by country and Topic by AI, data and privacy, competition or payments.

    Impact ratings and the "why it matters" notes are AIShorts editorial judgements, not legal advice. Items without a note are new and still waiting for review.

    Reading the data correctly

    Events vs measures. One law (a measure) moves through several events: introduced, passed, adopted, in force. Counts on this site are events, so one big law can add several.

    One law→IntroducedPassedAdoptedIn force

    Event date vs published date. Alerts are ordered by when DPA published a record. The event itself can be older, which is what "catalogued late" flags.

    Coverage. DPA has tracked daily since 2020 for the G20, the EU-27, Switzerland and selected Asia-Pacific and African jurisdictions. Elsewhere it records only major events, so a blank there doesn't prove nothing happened.

    Scope notes. US figures include state-level activity. The EU section covers Brussels-level rules only, not national laws. Lawsuits by public authorities appear when filed; private lawsuits appear only once ruled on. Counts by area can overlap, because one event can sit in more than one area.

    FAQ

    How fresh is the data?

    The Policy Desk refreshes it several times a day. Each page shows when it was last updated, and "Check now" fetches the latest copy without reloading.

    Why does "Check now" say I'm up to date?

    Nothing new has been published since the last refresh. The note under the buttons shows how old the feed is.

    Do I need an account?

    No. Everything here is free to read. Your "seen" list and filters are kept only in your own browser.

    Can I rely on this for compliance decisions?

    Use it to spot what to look at, then confirm with the official source (tap any item) and your legal adviser. Nothing here is legal advice.

    Something looks wrong. What should I do?

    Tap the item to check the DPA record. If our summary or rating is off, tell us through the contact page. Corrections are logged under our standards.
